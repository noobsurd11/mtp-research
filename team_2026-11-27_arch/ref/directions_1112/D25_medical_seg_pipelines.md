# D25: Self-configuring and modern medical segmentation pipelines

Scout: D25 literature agent, 13 Nov 2026. R = what a paper measured, E = what our files measured (from BRIEF), S = my judgement.

## Bottom line (S)
- The strongest evidence in this direction is negative for architecture hype and positive for pipeline configuration. nnU-Net Revisited (R): with 3D data trained from scratch, CNN U-Nets (nnU-Net, ResEnc M/L/XL, MedNeXt) beat every transformer, and U-Mamba's gain came from its residual U-Net. When the Mamba layers were switched off ("No-Mamba Base"), the scores did not change. U-Bench (R, 100 variants, 28 2D datasets): over 80% of variants are not significantly better than U-Net in-domain, and fundus/nuclei (thin, local structures) stagnate. Zero-shot (new-domain) gains are larger and more often significant, and long-range / hybrid models lead there.
- For us this splits cleanly. (a) Problem 1 (crack in-distribution) and problem 4 are most likely moved by the nnU-Net *recipe*: long schedule, SGD with high momentum, deep supervision, foreground oversampling, 5-fold module-grouped CV with fold ensembling, and a larger residual encoder. A new block is less likely to help. None of these recipe pieces has been tested here (BRIEF section 5 says "never varied" for schedules and optimizers, "never tried" for nnU-Net auto-configuration and k-fold selection). (b) Problem 3 (E1) is where long-range or hybrid encoders (Swin-UMamba, RWKV-UNet, nnWNet) have their best external evidence. Problem 5 (measurement) and the benchmark are served directly by nnU-Net Revisited's dataset-suitability analysis and by U-Bench's protocol.
- Main adaptation risk: nnU-Net's default augmentation rotates by up to 180 degrees and scales by 0.7 to 1.4 with interpolation. On 1 to 2 px crack labels, a 1 px shift costs 0.44 IoU (E). The house deliberately uses "label-safe" augmentation. Every nnU-Net arm should therefore be run twice, with default augmentation and with label-safe augmentation.

### Per-paper details (min experiment, overlap, cost)

1. **nnU-Net.** Min experiment: nnU-Net v2 `2d` on the module-grouped 5 folds, arms A (default augmentation) and B (label-safe augmentation); the 5 fold models are ensembled on test-49 and E1. Baseline: house 5-seed ensemble (test crack IoU 0.485 to 0.488 at argmax, relF1@2 0.897, inactive 0.456, mIoU_rob 0.583; E1 crack relF1@2 about 0.45, E1 inactive 0.28 with PF). Also compare the CV-mean of the house model trained on the same folds. Kill: if both arms are below the house ensemble by more than 0.012 crack IoU with no gain in relF1@2, inactive or E1, drop the recipe. Cost: about 10 GPU-h per fold for 1000 epochs at 512 px (S, rough), so 5 folds x 2 arms is about 100 GPU-h, which fits in 2 days on cv02. Overlap: none. BRIEF section 5 lists schedules, optimizers, k-fold selection and nnU-Net auto-configuration as never tried. Partial overlap: "Dice on 12 classes (worse)". nnU-Net's default Dice covers all classes, hence arm C.
2. **ResEnc presets (nnU-Net Revisited).** R: KiTS DSC 86.04 (org), 86.79 (M), 88.17 (L), 88.67 (XL); MedNeXt best on 5 of 6 datasets; U-Mamba Bot 91.79 against No-Mamba 91.89 on ACDC. Min experiment: ResEnc M against L on the same folds and recipe as row 1, label-safe arm. Kill: L is not above M by 0.01 crack IoU or 0.02 inactive IoU in CV mean. Cost about 150 GPU-h. Overlap: ResNet-50 U-Net++ was worse on inactive (E). That run differed in backbone source (ImageNet), decoder (U-Net++), recipe (20k Adam steps) and the fine-tune that erased layers 3 and 4, so it is not the same mechanism.
3. **MedNeXt.** Min experiment: MedNeXt-B k3, then UpKern to k5, in nnU-Net 2D, label-safe, 5 folds; baseline is the ResEnc M arm from row 2. Kill: no fold-mean gain in crack relF1@2 or E1 relF1@2. Cost about 200 GPU-h (R: MedNeXt is the slowest in Table 1, 68 to 233 h in 3D). Overlap: none.
4. **U-Bench.** Min experiment: the harness runs U-Net, Swin-UMamba, RWKV-UNet, MEGANet, UTANet, U-KAN and VM-UNet at 512 px, 1 channel, under one recipe, with one seed per fold. Rank them by test crack relF1@2 (in-domain) and E1 relF1@2 (zero-shot). Kill for the score line: no variant beats the house model on E1 relF1@2 by more than 0.03. Cost about 15 variants x 5 folds x 1.5 h, roughly 110 GPU-h. Overlap: none (BRIEF: no transformer or Mamba segmenter tried).
5. **Swin-UMamba.** Min experiment: Swin-UMamba with PF fine-tune against the house PF model, 3 seeds each, 20k steps; metric E1 crack relF1@2 and E1 inactive IoU. Kill: below +0.03 E1 relF1@2 with test crack IoU not worse than -0.01. Cost about 15 GPU-h. Overlap: the foundation-encoder screens were DINOv3/ConvNeXt, linear probes or a 4k-step fine-tune, so they do not close this.
6. **RWKV-UNet.** Same design as row 5, against house and PF. Kill line the same. Cost about 15 GPU-h. Overlap: none.
7. **nnWNet.** Min experiment: nnWNet trainer on the same folds as rows 1 and 2, label-safe; compare with nnU-Net org and ResEnc M. Kill: CV-mean crack relF1@2 not above ResEnc M. Cost about 60 GPU-h. Overlap: none.
8. **U-Mamba.** Min experiment: U-Mamba Bot against No-Mamba Base on 5 folds, metric E1 relF1@2. Kill: difference within fold SD (R predicts this outcome). Cost about 60 GPU-h. Overlap: none.
9. **Auto-nnU-Net.** Min experiment: one PriorBand run with a 300 GPU-h budget over the space above. The incumbent is evaluated once on test-49 and E1 against row 1 arm B. Kill: under +0.01 CV objective over the default config. Overlap: none. All loss and optimizer sweeps in section 5 were one-at-a-time on the house recipe.
10. **Mamba-Sea.** Min experiment: add sequence-wise style augmentation to the row 5 or row 6 bottleneck and train with leave-one-source-out folds. Metric E1 crack relF1@2 and E1 inactive. Kill: under +0.02 E1 relF1@2. Cost about 30 GPU-h. Overlap: "intensity normalisation (fail)" and "per-panel BN adaptation (fail)" were inference-time. This is train-time feature-style perturbation, so it is still open.
11. **TransUNet (MedIA).** Min experiment: TransUNet encoder plus query decoder, PF, 3 seeds, against house PF. Metric test crack IoU, inactive IoU and E1. Kill: no metric above the house by 1 seed SD. Cost about 20 GPU-h.
12 to 15. Benchmark-suite members. One run per fold under the shared protocol; no separate kill line.

## Benchmark design points taken from this direction (for the user's benchmark vision)
- nnU-Net Revisited R1 to R4: isolate confounders (pretraining, ensembling, compute), use a standardised well-configured baseline (nnU-Net plus ResEnc L), use 5-fold CV with a separate held-out test, and report all classes. Its SD-ratio test can tell whether test-49 strict crack IoU separates methods better than seed noise does. BRIEF problem 5 says it may not.
- U-Bench: paired tests against the baseline per dataset, a zero-shot axis (our E1), and an efficiency axis (U-Score). This fits the EL benchmark directly: test-49 in-domain, E1 zero-shot, with FLOPs/FPS for edge deployment.
- GP-VMxSMA: linear mixed-effects analysis with fold and dataset (for us, fold and source) as random effects. It also includes general-purpose models such as SegFormer so the benchmark is not medical-only.
- nnU-Net itself: a self-configuring baseline that any EL paper can rerun from a splits file. That makes it the natural "reference method" for the EL benchmark.

## Notable papers rejected
- Primus (arXiv 2503.01835): pure transformer for 3D; no 2D variant; 3D-only evidence.
- MedNeXt-v2 (arXiv 2512.17774): 3D large-scale supervised pretraining; weights do not transfer to 2D EL.
- SegMamba (doi:10.1007/978-3-031-72111-3_54), Taming Mambas (arXiv 2410.15496): 3D only.
- Auto3DSeg (covered inside nnU-Net Revisited): underperformed nnU-Net out of the box in 5 of 6 datasets (R).
- Mamba-UNet, LightM-UNet, LKM-UNet, VM-UNet-V2, KM-UNet, MedVKAN, xLSTM-UNet: same family as rows 5, 8, 12 and 13 without a stronger controlled result; add them to the suite only if cheap.
- Revisiting model scaling U-Net benchmark (doi:10.1038/s41598-025-15617-1): 3D; ResEnc L/XL already covers scaling.
- CrackMamba (doi:10.1016/j.autcon.2024.105845), MixerCSeg (arXiv 2603.01361), task-aligned crack modelling (arXiv 2605.31048): crack-specific architectures; leave them to the crack/thin-structure scout.
- Camyla (arXiv 2604.10696): autonomous research agent, not a method.
- MedSegBench (doi:10.1038/s41597-024-04159-2): dataset collection, no method; useful only as a template for benchmark packaging.

## Gaps
- search_snippets was rate-limited every time, so I could not pull 2D-specific ablations of deep supervision or oversampling ratio. These numbers are still missing: what deep supervision and the oversampling ratio do on 2D thin structures, and what 1000 epochs buys over shorter schedules in 2D.
- Whether the nnU-Net v2 spatial augmentation interpolates segmentation labels with nearest neighbour (likely) or one-hot linear interpolation was not verified. It matters for 1 to 2 px cracks.
- 2D support and its configuration in the MedNeXt repo are unverified. The U-KAN, TransUNet and GP-VMxSMA code URLs are unverified.
- The nnWNet DRIVE/CREMI numbers against nnU-Net were not retrieved (main-paper tables not fetched).
- No paper runs nnU-Net or these architectures on EL images. The closest domains are DRIVE (vessels), CREMI (membranes) and CHASE (vessels, via CrackMamba).
- Sub-direction worth its own search: leave-one-source-out and domain-generalisation recipes inside nnU-Net (for example nnU-Net with BigAug or style augmentation) aimed at E1.
