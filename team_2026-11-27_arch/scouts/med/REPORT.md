## Bottom line
1. **No new architecture in this family has controlled evidence of beating a tuned U-Net on small 2D thin-structure data by a margin that matters for us.** The only controlled thin-structure 2D comparison I found with all baselines retrained under one recipe is SA-UNetv2: DRIVE F1 82.82 vs 81.30 (U-Net) and 81.18 (U-Net++). KGS-UNet claims +1.5% IoU/F1 over 70+ variants on DRIVE/CHASE (abstract only). Both gains are small, and both are on 20-image vessel sets, not on our tolerant crack metric.
2. **The useful transfers are recipe items, not backbones:** per-class postprocessing tuned on cross-validation predictions (Extending nnU-Net; Scaling nnU-Net for CBCT), augmentation chosen per class or task (CBCT mirroring result; the ultrasound augmentation study), and metric-aware selection plus heterogeneous ensembles (Why is the Winner the Best).
3. **The best new evidence on our two-sided problem (E1 recall vs 1-2 px precision) is a 2026 controlled study (arXiv 2611.14519).** On every external site, DINOv2 + LoRA beat nnU-Net-recipe U-Nets on DSC. Within the training distribution, U-Nets matched it, and DINOv2 more often missed small lesions entirely and drew coarser boundaries. This argues for keeping the full-resolution CNN and adding foundation-model features as a side input (LUMOS-style), not swapping encoders. Our A8 KILL (DINOv3 ConvNeXt-T as the encoder) fits this reading.
4. Generalist medical and microscopy models (MedSAM2, BiomedParse, SAT, UniverSeg, Cellpose/StarDist/CellSAM/µSAM) are prompt-based, 3D or instance-blob models. None reports anything on 1-2 px lines. I rated them 0 to 1. The one microscopy idea worth keeping is the Cellpose replay result for few-shot transfer (arXiv 2511.04803).

## Top 10
Failure modes: **R** = recall on unseen modules (E1, 0.42), **H** = half-cut layout, **P** = thin-line precision (width and false-crack guards), **I** = rare inactive.

1. **arXiv 2208.10791, Extending nnU-Net Is All You Need (NEW, full text).** The AMOS-2022 winner gained over the nnU-Net baseline from a residual encoder, a larger batch and patch, a 15-model ensemble (5 folds × 3 configurations, softmax averaging) and **per-class postprocessing chosen on 5-fold CV predictions**: connected-component filtering and minimum-size rules with a "rate" safety margin. For us: tune a minimum crack-component length and a minimum inactive area on the out-of-fold predictions of the 5 cv5v3 folds, per class. Maps to **P** (false crack pixels on crack-free cells, which is a guard) and **I** (spurious inactive blobs). Config-only, CPU-only. Risk: deleting short true cracks lowers relR on E1; select the cutoff on relF1@2, never on IoU.
2. **arXiv 2411.17213, Scaling nnU-Net for CBCT Segmentation (NEW, full text).** On ToothFairy2 with ResEnc L: disabling only left/right mirroring raised F-case Dice 0.7445 → 0.9083, while disabling all mirroring was worse. 1500 vs 1000 epochs gave a minor gain. MultiTalent pretraining gave no gain after postprocessing. Per-class, per-metric component cutoffs were tuned on 5-fold CV, and the authors warn that those CV numbers are optimistic. Lessons: (a) audit our D4 flips/rotations per class for label safety (any class defined by position relative to busbars, edges or cut lines); (b) the same CV-tuned cutoff arm as #1, with a held-out split for the cutoff, which they say they lacked. Maps to **P**, **H** (a half-cut edge is a positional cue).
3. **arXiv 2611.14519, Foundation Model Adaptation versus Task-Specific Training (NEW, full text).** DINOv2 + LoRA r=32 against nnU-Net-recipe U-Net and ResU-Net on polyp, breast ultrasound and dermoscopy. Out of distribution, DINOv2 led on all 8 external sets (deficits of the U-Nets −0.008 to −0.107 DSC), mostly through precision in polyp and breast. In distribution, the U-Net matched (0.8803 vs 0.8780), already at 119 breast images. DINOv2 predicted nothing on up to 13 of the polyp images (median missed-lesion area 0.86% of the image), and its boundary was coarser at 224 px (16×16 patch grid). More data closed the in-domain gap but not the out-of-distribution one. For us this predicts that an FM encoder helps **R** but hurts **P**, which is the A8 outcome. It also documents selection-criterion and mismatched-comparison artefacts relevant to our protocol.
4. **arXiv 2603.01115, LUMOS (NEW, abstract only).** A frozen VFM generates spatial priors ("Pathfinder") that guide a conventional medical segmentor ("Inspiror"); DINO gave stable gains when the backbone was matched. This is the hybrid that #3 points to: keep HRNet-W18 + U-Net++ at full resolution for **P**, and add frozen DINO features for module-level context aimed at **R** and **H**. New head. Risk: abstract only; Dino U-Net (P043) and SAM2-UNet (P002) already cover nearby ground.
5. **arXiv 2511.04803, Redundancy and Forgetting with Cellpose (NEW, abstract only).** 10% of the Cyto data (chosen by dataset quantisation) saturates performance. Cross-domain fine-tuning causes forgetting, and replaying a DQ-chosen 5-10% of source data restores the source while full replay hinders target adaptation. Domain ordering mattered. For the few-shot track (pool + labelled cells of a new module type), this means replaying a small, diverse slice of the 532-cell pool instead of mixing all of it in. Maps to **R** on new layouts and **H** if half-cut cells are the new domain. Config-only. It needs a new unseen set, because E1 is now training data.
6. **arXiv 2509.11774, SA-UNetv2 (NEW, full text).** Cross-scale spatial attention in all skips plus a 0.5 BCE + 0.5 differentiable MCC loss, with all baselines retrained using the same block and loss. DRIVE (20 training images) F1 82.82 vs U-Net 81.30, U-Net++ 81.18; STARE 82.81 vs 79.74 / 79.56; 0.26M params. Two cheap pieces for us: an MCC term on the crack channel (it counts FP and FN symmetrically, as relF1 does) and skip spatial attention. Maps to **P** and possibly **R**. Caveats: the gain over SA-UNet is +0.38 F1; the MCC loss was not ablated alone; we have seen no decoder effect so far.
7. **arXiv 2304.06229, ICI loss (NEW, abstract only).** An instance-wise term weights each connected component equally, plus a centre-of-instance term. On ATLAS R2.0 it beat Dice loss by 1.7-3.7% and blob loss by 0.6-5.0% Dice. Missed E1 cracks tend to be whole faint segments, and inactive regions are rare instances, so this maps to **R** and **I**. It is a variant to try only if CC-DiceCE (P115, RUN_NOW) shows a signal; otherwise skip.
8. **arXiv 2501.13193, Revisiting Data Augmentation for Ultrasound Images (NEW, full text, TMLR).** On 7 segmentation tasks the best single augmentation (brightness) averaged only +2.03%. Effects were task-specific (Gaussian noise +2.77% on one task, −40.41% on another). Growing the augmentation set first helped, then hurt. TrivialAugment beat no augmentation on 6 of 7 tasks. Supports a one-at-a-time augmentation screen per class (crack vs inactive) rather than importing nnU-Net's default set. Config-only. Maps to **R** (photometric augmentations for contrast shift) and **P**.
9. **doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02595-5, Cellpose3 (NEW, abstract only; no open-access PDF).** Restoration networks trained so that a frozen segmenter segments their output well, with a perceptual term, across many datasets; it reports large out-of-the-box gains on noisy, blurry and undersampled images. A segmentation-aware restorer trained on our five sources plus the 11,938 unlabelled cells could normalise contrast before the segmenter, aimed at **R**. Risk: per-source calibration and intensity normalisation already failed here, and a restorer can erase 1-2 px lines (**P**). New pipeline; lower priority.
10. **arXiv 2303.17719, Why is the Winner the Best? (NEW, full text).** Across 80 ISBI/MICCAI 2021 challenges, winners ensembled over seeds, splits and heterogeneous models 5.6×, 1.7× and 2.5× more often than others, tuned hyperparameters on the challenge metric, and spent twice the time on failure analysis. In-domain pretraining was rated more important than ImageNet. No factor survived multiple-testing correction. For us: select checkpoints, SWAD window and thresholds on relF1@2 directly, and if ensembles are revisited, make them heterogeneous (for example HRNet + ResNet-34 encoders), since our same-architecture ensembles (PF2E, SKR2E) failed. Maps to protocol and **P**/**R** indirectly.

Next in line (fit 2): KGS-UNet (doi:10.1109/ijcnn69854.2026.11728422; small structures, limited data, 70+ variant benchmark, abstract only), nnLoGoNet (doi:10.1038/s41598-026-50020-4; 2D vessels in an nnU-Net trainer with Skeleton Recall), and the MELBA SAM fine-tuning study (arXiv 2404.09957: fine-tuned SAM only slightly above nnU-Net; PEFT in encoder and decoder is best; full fine-tune of ViT-H collapsed, about −35% DSC on one MRI set). The last is the guide to use if SAM2-UNet (P002) is run.
