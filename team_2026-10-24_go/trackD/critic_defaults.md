## 1. Direction 3: labelled-set experiment (main contrast settled)
**Decision on the R1 vs R2 contrast.** R1's three-arm design is adopted. It contains R2's contrast at no extra cost, so both questions are answered from the same 27 runs.

**Decision rule (per contrast, per primary metric).** Unit = draw. For each draw, the mean over its 3 seeds. Δ = mean of draw-means (B) − (A); threshold = max(0.01, 2·sqrt(var_A/3 + var_B/3)) with var over the 3 draw-means. BETTER / WORSE / n.d. as rule 1. Also report the seed-level rule-1 arithmetic (9 v 9) as descriptive, so the reader sees how much draw variance adds.

## 2. Direction 4 in SYNTHESIS: frozen-encoder probe per source (inference only, ~1 GPU-h)
- **Question:** does any frozen pretrained encoder separate crack on BMRK (and on the 40 always-missed components) better than our fine-tuned A5c?
- **Encoders:** ImageNet ResNet-34 (our init), ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-T, DINOv2 ViT-S/14; add a DINOv3 ConvNeXt only if a checkpoint is already local or cached (no new large downloads without asking).
- **Probe:** linear 1×1 head on frozen features at stride 4 (bilinear-upsampled concat of stride 4/8 where available), trained on the **same 23 labelled 5% cells**, revised masks, 2 probe seeds, fixed recipe as P4. Val is not used.
- **Report per source (test, plus the 448-cell pool with hidden masks):** crack AP, relaxed recall at the probe's own argmax, and recall at matched precision to A5c+LA on that source. A5c and A5c+LA per-source AP need the same inference pass.
- **Decision:** any encoder with BMRK crack AP > A5c's BMRK AP by ≥ 0.05 (2 probe seeds agree) keeps the encoder/distillation route open and gates MIM out; none does, MIM stays the remaining encoder bet. The 40 components bound the pooled effect at ≤ +0.02 to +0.03.
