EL crack segmentation: what the agent teams did and found

Seven days of critic-gated agent teams on semi-supervised segmentation of rare defects (crack, inactive) in electroluminescence images of solar cells. Two published SSL methods were adapted and tested, the failure was diagnosed, a best model was built, and the question of how to use the unlabelled data was researched from scratch. Every number below was recomputed by an independent critic agent before it was accepted.

Conclusions

The seven things this week established, strongest first.

  1. Pseudo-label SSL helps only when labels are scarce. At 5% labels the best recipe (BsM5: Mean Teacher with hard pseudo-labels, CutMix, separate BatchNorm) beats supervised on crack IoU (+0.029) and inactive IoU (+0.107). At 20, 50 and 100% labels a crack gain of +0.02 or more is ruled out, and +0.01 at 100%.

  2. The two recent SOTA methods did not transfer. ALHVR (ICCV 2025), adapted to EL, is worse than supervised on all three primary metrics and halves inactive recall. SKCDF's balanced head (CVPR 2025) is n.d.; it corrects a class collapse our models do not have.

  3. Labels beat SSL at equal budget. Adding 12 crack-rich labelled cells (chosen with ground truth) gives crack IoU 0.352, against 0.331 for the best SSL run and 0.324 for 12 random cells.

  4. The best model is an ensemble, not a new training trick. Five seeds of the 100%-label U-Net++/ResNet-34, averaged, with a small crack bias. Six training changes (Dice variants, ResNet-50, extra E-SCDD cells, 2x resolution) all failed the val inclusion rule.

  5. Strict crack IoU is near its labelling ceiling. A perfect detector 1 px off in position or width scores 0.48 to 0.60, and we are at 0.49. 70% of the remaining IoU loss lies within 2 px of the right place.

  6. The real gap is a defect context we never labelled. On unseen modules, 75% of crack pixels sit in half-cut cells with scuff streaks, where the model finds 13%. Training has no half-cut cells and has always ignored scuff. The 12k unlabelled pool barely contains such cells, so it cannot fix this.

  7. The user's own intuition was never tested. Everything run was label propagation. Learning features from the unlabelled cells first (masked-image or contrastive pretraining) was ranked low and dropped. One bounded test is proposed (1 to 5 GPU-h pretraining).

What the adaptation involved

  • ALHVR-EL: dual networks with regions Ω1 to Ω3, DTCT loss, CG-CPCL prototypes at the full-resolution decoder output, noise-only perturbation (no geometric aug that would move thin cracks), λ 0.1 with warmup 2k to 4k. CPS and CPS-masked baselines isolate each module. Arms AL5, ALd5 (no CPCL), ALdh5 (λ 0.3), CPS5, CPSm5.
  • Why it fails: 65 to 70% of GT crack and 56 to 60% of GT inactive fall in Ω1, where ALHVR applies no unlabelled loss. Its sharpened-MSE plus entropy loss on Ω2/Ω3 teaches background on inactive regions. Plain cross pseudo-labels (CPS) do not harm.
  • SKCDF ABSH: auxiliary balanced head on the decoupled recipe, with and without a 2 px negative band around cracks. ABSH targets classes stuck at 0 Dice, which we do not have. Without the band (BsDBn5) it matches a free logit bias on IoU.

S-a: the free-bias yardstick

Logit adjustment (subtract τ·log class prior, τ = 1, prior from the 23 labelled cells) costs no training. It lifts A5c crack IoU 0.304 → 0.319 and BsD5 0.328 → 0.341. Any SSL claim on crack must beat this. With the bias given to both sides, BsM5's crack gain still passes (+0.037; +0.035 without twins and the noisy cell).

Why standard SSL fails here

24 Oct team: data side (R1), method side (R2), critic C. team_2026-10-24_why/SYNTHESIS.md.

Measured

  • Crack belief is bimodal: 57% of GT crack px score below 0.001 and 35% at or above 0.95. Pseudo-labels can only act on the 8 to 19% in between.
  • The crack gain is source-bound (without twins): CFVS +0.047, BMRK +0.002, ARTS −0.030.
  • Labelled coverage: at 5%, ARTS has 0 labelled crack cells and BMRK 2. SSL cannot spread a pattern it never saw.
  • About 38% of pool crack is a true miss more than 2 px from any prediction. No training rule moved that by more than 0.046.
  • Inactive is the counter-case: supervised under-extends regions (precision 0.77, recall 0.28); self-training extends them (recall 0.43).

Reasoned and ruled out

  • Self-training spreads labels through neighbourhoods the teacher already gets right (expansion assumption, arXiv 2010.03622). Faint BMRK cracks overlap background.
  • Published gains come mostly from weak or from-scratch baselines, or from blob-like classes. Our pretrained, thresholded, separate-BN base had already taken the cheap part.
  • Oliver et al. (arXiv 1804.09170): we fail two realistic-SSL conditions, a pool i.i.d. with the labels and a val set large enough to tune (6 inactive val cells).
  • Not causes: pool size (28x pool adds nothing), the twins and the noisy cell (they distort measurement, not mechanism).

Unlabelled data: how to read the result

27 Oct research team: lit (literature, Research MCP), evalx (measurement), ideas (methods), judge (critic). 2 to 3 challenge rounds each. team_2026-10-27_unlabelled/SYNTHESIS.md.

The user's question: ~480 labelled cells give a result, ~12k unlabelled cells exist; learning features from them should improve scores, so why did nothing work? The answer: what we ran spreads labels the model already has. It cannot invent a crack the model scores at 0.001, and the pool does not contain the cells the model fails on. Feature learning itself was never tested.

What the measurements say

  • The test can see small crack gains. On the 49 clean cells the detectable crack effect at 100% labels is about 0.010 to 0.012. External-pool SSL at 100% (Sx100) measured −0.002 [−0.007, +0.003]. The old "+0.028" came entirely from one supervised seed failing on the noisy cell.
  • Inactive is where the test is too small: 16 inactive test cells; a real +0.01 to +0.03 inactive gain would be invisible (detectable 0.032 at 100%, 0.084 at 5%).
  • Labelling ceiling: of FINAL's crack IoU loss, 70% is within 2 px of GT, 18% missed cracks, 11% false alarms. One cell labelled twice agrees with itself at crack IoU 0.53 (E-SCDD masks).
  • Scope: the 20 to 100% null covers the plain recipe (Cs). BsM was never run at 50 to 100%; at 20% its crack gain +0.013 [−0.002, +0.033] is not ruled out.

What the literature says

  • Same-encoder SSL gains: +6 to +11 mIoU at 92 to 316 labels, +0.3 to +1.6 mIoU at full labels with a non-matching pool (UniMatch V2, doi:10.1109/tpami.2025.3528453). Our 5% gain fits the first, our 100% null fits the second.
  • Thin-structure masked-image-modelling gains are measured against training from scratch or across architectures, never against ImageNet init with the same architecture. Closest industrial case (7k unlabelled, 625 labelled, thin wires): in-domain MAE lost the wire class to an ImageNet ResNet-18, 43.0 vs 59.1 IoU (arXiv 2605.12430).
  • Torpey (arXiv 2402.17611), the only EL test: in-domain SimCLR 0.517 mIoU vs ImageNet-supervised 0.508, but behind ImageNet-SimCLR (0.534) and COCO (0.544); its pool was also mismatched.
  • No EL or crack paper shows an SSL gain on pixel-level crack over a tuned ImageNet baseline. SAM-family models fail on thin, low-contrast structures.

  • The model paints the scuff streak as crack (about 8.7k px per run) and misses the real cracks branching off it (median 12 px from the streak, so not an outline convention).
  • Training has 0 half-cut cells and only 1 cell with ≥ 500 scuff px; scuff has always been scored as ignore.
  • None of the 32 unseen-module crack cells comes from a module in the 12k pool. The pool's 1,152 half-cells carry only small thin cracks. No pool SSL arm ever moved half-cut recall by more than +0.023.
  • Inside our own test, cells from unseen modules lose almost no crack (0.486 vs 0.499) but lose inactive (recall 0.43 vs 0.92).

Test set and metric: keep, then add

  • Keep the 50 cells for continuity; add the clean set as co-primary, fixed by an image-only noise rule before any new run.
  • E-SCDD's official test is unusable: 48 of its 82 cells are in our train.
  • Keep the 106 unseen-module cells sealed, reported as two readouts: scuff + half-cut, and the rest.
  • For future protocols, judge crack by segments found and 2 px recall at a fixed 90% precision set on val, with IoU beside it. Past verdicts stay under their locked metrics.
  • Module-grouped 5-fold CV over 693 cells (74 with inactive) only if inactive at 50%+ labels becomes the question: about 100 GPU-h per SSL contrast.

What is already a thesis contribution

  1. A tight, measured null for pseudo-label SSL at 20 to 100% labels on EL crack, with the old +0.028 traced to one seed on one corrupted cell.
  2. SSL's benefit depends on label scarcity and on the class: at 5%, crack +0.029 and inactive +0.107; inactive keeps gaining to 20%, crack fades by 10 to 20%. Fits the expansion account.
  3. Two 2025 SOTA methods adapted to EL with mechanisms for their failure: ALHVR-EL worse (no loss where the defects are, inactive suppressed); SKCDF head n.d. (no collapse to fix).
  4. The free logit-bias yardstick: SSL crack claims must beat logit adjustment, and BsM5 still does (+0.037).
  5. Test-set leakage found and handled: 7 twin cells hold 39.5% of test crack; claims reported with and without them.
  6. Crack IoU has a labelling ceiling (0.48 to 0.60 for a 1 px offset); capture metrics should judge "catch almost all cracks".
  7. "Unseen modules" decomposed: the crack loss is a missing defect context (scuff + half-cut), not a generic domain shift.
  8. Labels beat SSL at equal budget for crack (oracle-selected), and targeted crack cells beat random cells.

Limits on all of it: one split, 50 test cells (49 clean), 10 unseen-module inactive cells, cv02 only, untuned SSL recipes, oracle selection in Track D.
